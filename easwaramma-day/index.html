Easwaramma Day

A celebration of the sacred ideal of motherhood, and the largest offering our children make each year.

Why we celebrate Easwaramma Day

May 6th every year is celebrated as Easwaramma Day, commemorating the anniversary of the passing away of the saintly lady who was blessed to be mother to the Avatar of the Kali age. Bhagawan exhorts His devotees, especially children, to revere the mother as God — and so this day is a celebration of the sacred ideal of motherhood.

Easwaramma was very fond of children, and was one of the pioneers of the Bal Vikas movement. The day is therefore also celebrated as Bal Vikas Day, when hundreds of Bal Vikas children gather to present their cultural programmes and imbibe the highest spiritual lessons from their Divine Mother.

The programme

Nava Vidha Bhakthi — the nine paths of devotion

The 2026 programme took the nine paths of devotion as its theme — Sravanam, Keertanam, Smaranam, Padasevanam, Archanam, Vandanam, Dasyam, Sakhyam and Atmanivedanam — each presented by a different group of children and set out in dioramas around the hall.

Preparing it asks a real commitment: rehearsals every Saturday afternoon and Sunday for four to five weeks before the programme, plus a full run-through on the Friday evening. Alumni return each year to help the younger children with transitions and costume changes.

It teaches skills that outlast the day itself — self-confidence, patience, teamwork and communication. Many of our alumni look back on Easwaramma Day rehearsals with real fondness.
