Swami’s 100th Birthday Celebrations


Swami says seva should happen naturally and spontaneously. It should occur naturally to us to serve others. He often quotes this line from our scriptures - “Paropakaarartham idam sariram” – this body is meant to help others.


Our Bal Vikas families are engaged in multiple seva activities to celebrate Swami’s 100th birthday.

Sai Aashraya Baby Gift Kits

Our families have sponsored 100 gift kits containing baby outfits and cloth diapers as well as lovingly crocheted sweaters and hats to the newborns arriving for treatment at Sai Aashraya’s facilities.

 

Care Kits Seva

Many of you are aware of the kits we assemble every year for people without fixed addresses on the streets of Dallas. Personal hygiene items are sponsored by our families and the kits are assembled and distributed by our 10th graders during the holidays to celebrate Swami’s birthday. This year too, these kits will be distributed with all your support. Swami encourages devotees to perform seva activities in their own backyards and help the needy in their own communities. This annual seva gives us all the opportunity to practice this principle.

 

Folding Paper Cranes

Two-year old Sadako Sasaki was at home in Hiroshima, Japan, on August 6, 1945, when the US dropped the Little Boy atomic bomb over her city. Sadako survived the bombing of Hiroshima but passed away ten years later from leukemia. Japanese folklore says that a crane can live for a thousand years, and a person who folds an origami crane for each year of a crane’s life will have their wish granted. Sadako folded 1,300 origami cranes before her death in 1955.

Sadako has inspired generations of children to fold paper cranes to have their wishes fulfilled.

Swami has said that seva can be performed by our thoughts and words too, not just by our actions.  When He introduced the prayer “Samastah Lokah Sukhino Bhavantu”, he said that when we pray for the whole world, it has immense effect on our minds and the atmosphere surrounding us.

 

So with a wish for global peace, our Bal Vikas children and families are folding 1000 paper cranes. Watch out for creative displays of these works of art from our kids when you attend the 100th birthday celebration on Nov 23. And mentally join them in the prayer for world peace!

Significance & Origin of Akhanda Bhajans

During World War II, a small group of Sai Baba’s early devotees from Bangalore began holding weekly bhajans to stay connected with Him. In 1946, they organized the first 24-hour Akhanda Bhajan, which Swami personally attended, blessing the food so it miraculously never ran out. The event’s success led to it becoming an annual tradition that Swami supported for 28 years. In 1974, He proclaimed that the Akhanda Bhajan should spread globally, transforming a humble village gathering into a worldwide spiritual movement of love and devotion.

The Divine Power of Group Devotional Singing

Swami has repeatedly emphasized the profound significance of group devotional singing.


His words beautifully capture its essence : "You must sing the names aloud, full-throated, as far as the voice can reach. Only then the Divine will respond in full measure and shower His grace. No one will go to the rescue of a drowning man if his cries are feeble. Only when he cries aloud at the top of his voice will the cries be heard and people will rush to save him." (Sri Sathya Sai Speaks Vol. 25/Ch. 08, Mar. 03, 1992)


Our center holds bhajans from 11:20 AM to 12 noon on Bal Vikas class days, and we warmly encourage all students and parents to participate.


 

Veda Classes

Another parent of one of our alumni, Mahendraji, has been teaching Vedas to Bal Vikas children for over a decade. Kids in 4th grade and above can participate.


Classes are held such that they can attend during the time when their classes are not in session. Swami has emphasized the importance of Veda Samrakshanam in many discourses as well as by being an exemplar. Our Veda Classes are inspired by Swami as an effort to sustain our ancient scriptures by training our kids to chant them with discipline while spreading positive vibrations even as they learn and practice.

Bringing Presence back to Parenting

Truly being present with our kids may require skills we haven’t developed.

This article from Psychology Today reminds us that genuine connection comes from undivided attention, not multitasking. Research shows that just a few minutes of mindful, positive engagement—using PRIDE skills like praise, reflection, and imitation—can greatly strengthen the parent-child bond.
